# Domain Credibility Review — Advisor Desktop

Reviewer stance: wealth-management practitioner (RIA advisory + portfolio ops + compliance).
Repo reviewed read-only at `/Users/joellewis/code/advisor-desktop`, commit `72dad62`, plus rendered screenshots.
Authority for every finding is the finance skill library; skills are cited by name per finding.

Convention used throughout: **confidently wrong** = the maths, the rule, or the label is incorrect and a practitioner would call it an error. **Simplified** = defensible shortcut for a demo. I only flag simplifications when they are *mislabelled* as something rigorous.

---

## 1. Verdict

**The surface is excellent and the domain vocabulary is broad enough to fool a generalist. It does not survive a practitioner opening two screens and comparing them.**

What is genuinely good and worth saying out loud:
- The **entity model is right**: household → client → account → position → tax lot, with UMA sleeves, taxable/tax-deferred/tax-free treatment, inherited IRA, SIMPLE IRA, 529, multi-custodian, multi-currency. Most demos stop at "portfolio".
- The **workflow inventory is right**: drift → rebalance wizard → pre-trade compliance → OMS → reconciliation → billing → archive. That is the actual shape of an advisory ops stack.
- Several details are correct in a way that only comes from real research: the **Uniform Lifetime Table factor 26.5 for age 73** and the **April 1 of the following year** first-RMD deadline (`nbas.ts:53`); **SMH as a non-substantially-identical replacement for INTC** (`nbas.ts:34`); the **mutual fund forward-pricing notice** ("orders execute at the next calculated NAV (typically 4:00 PM ET). No limit orders or time-in-force options are available", `MutualFundTradeFields.tsx:164`); **WORM archive with `retentionPolicy: '7 years (SEC 17a-4)'`** (`archives.ts`); realistic **expense ratios** and **institutional minimums** (PIMIX $1,000,000, `trading.ts:39`); internally consistent **crypto market-cap ÷ supply = price** (`trading.ts:70-79`).

**Where it breaks first** — in order of how fast a practitioner hits it:

1. **The number that anchors the whole product is unit-wrong on the front page of the growth module.** `EFFECTIVE FEE 0.85 bps` (book screenshot). 0.85 bps is 0.0085%. The firm is displaying a fee schedule 100× smaller than it charges. Two clicks away, Billing says `Avg Fee Rate 88 bps`. Nobody in this industry misses that.
2. **Household AUM does not equal the sum of its accounts** for 5 of 13 households — Chen-Wong is stated at **$43.5M managed** against **$30.3M** of actual account value once FX is applied (44% overstated). The household roll-up and the book roll-up disagree by **$14.6M (16%)** on a $91M book.
3. **The concentration module flags VTI, SPY and AGG as IPS breaches** because it applies a 10% single-*name* limit to broad index ETFs. An advisor's first reaction is "this tool doesn't know what an ETF is."
4. **The wash-sale calendar is anchored on purchase dates, not loss sales** — conceptually inverted, and the header says "30-Day" when the window is 61 days.
5. **Every account shows identical risk metrics**: beta 0.92, Sharpe 1.34, and `VaR (95%) $485K` — the same $485K on Nancy Taylor's $100K IRA as on the $12M Chen Trust.

The pattern is consistent: **presentation-layer fluency, calculation-layer absence.** Almost nothing is computed from the position data. Attribution, risk, stress, sensitivity, Monte Carlo, benchmark returns and rebalance trades are all hardcoded constants that ignore the account. Where the app *does* compute (drift, HHI, concentration, cost-basis comparison, book roll-up) the arithmetic is mostly correct but the *definitions* are wrong.

A showcase audience will forgive a demo for having fake data. It will not forgive fake data that **contradicts itself on the same screen** — and this app does that on the dashboard (14 vs 20 pending actions; "2 Critical" vs "3 time-sensitive"; $1.3M vs $420K net flows), on Billing ($182K vs $199K for Q1 2026), and across performance (the same book reports YTD returns of 3.2%, 6.5%, 12.3% and 14.7% in four different modules).

## 4. Terminology & workflow gaps

### Terminology that is wrong or inconsistent

- **"30-Day Wash Sale Window"** (`WashSaleCalendarView.tsx:23`, `compliance.ts:159`, `oms.ts:151`). The window is **61 days**. The code comment at `TaxManagementPage.tsx:128` says 61; the header says 30. Per `tax-loss-harvesting`, the 30-days-*before* leg is the half practitioners most often forget — and this app forgets it in the label while (accidentally) implementing ±30 in `getWashSaleWindow`.
- **"Alpha"** used for simple excess return while beta is displayed beside it (`pms.ts:250-256`). Should be "Excess Return" / "Active Return". `performance-reporting`.
- **"Tax-loss harvesting window"** used as the rule name for a wash-sale check (`oms.ts:54`). Two different concepts.
- **"Effective fee 0.85 bps"** — unit error, and "effective fee" should be asset-weighted, not a simple average (Billing's "88 bps").
- **"Organic growth 3.2%"** is gross new assets. Organic growth is net of withdrawals and attrition (1.5%).
- **"Flat" fee type** used for a flat *rate*. `fee-billing` / `fee-disclosure`.
- **"Drift"** stored as an absolute value, discarding over/under-weight direction; and "drift tolerance" defined per model but never used as a **band**. `rebalancing` distinguishes absolute (±3–5%) from relative (±20–25%) bands — the app supports neither properly.
- **"Revenue Concentration HHI"** computed on AUM shares.
- **"SEC best practice recommends annual IPS reviews"** (`nbas.ts:144`) — there is no SEC rule requiring IPS review. Attributing a firm practice to a regulator is the kind of thing a CCO strikes immediately.
- **"Compliance requires source-of-funds documentation for transactions over $100K"** (`nbas.ts:165`) — no such federal threshold. CTR is $10,000 for currency; the wire Travel Rule is $3,000. A $100K trigger is a legitimate *firm policy* and should say so. Note that the FinCEN RIA AML rule (effective 1 Jan 2026) makes this genuinely topical for a Feb-2026-dated demo — worth getting right.
- **"Model targets 5% NVDA weight"** (`nbas.ts:40`) — models in this app are asset-class-level; no model contains a security-level target.
- **"Settlement: T+1 for equities and ETFs"** is rendered as static text on **every** ticket including mutual funds and bonds (`SingleTradeView.tsx:431`, `TradeTicketDialog.tsx:180`).

Terminology handled **well** and worth keeping: TWR labelled on the performance chart and column header; held-away vs managed AUM as distinct fields; UMA/sleeve; specific_id / fifo / average_cost per account; RMD, inherited IRA, SIMPLE IRA, SEP IRA; restricted list; wash sale; TLH; drift; model governance with versioning and Investment Committee approval.

### Workflow fidelity gaps

**Rebalance wizard** (`select → preview → review → execute`). The shape is right; the controls are not.
- **No second-person review anywhere.** One advisor selects, previews, clicks past compliance and executes. The codebase already has a `supervisorOverride` pattern (`nba.ts:104-124`) — it was simply never applied to trading. Per `rebalancing` Step 7 and `order-management-advisor`, block trades across accounts require an allocation policy and a fair-allocation check; neither exists.
- **Warnings never block.** `ComplianceReviewStep.tsx:128-135` prints *"Compliance warnings detected. Review before proceeding."* and leaves the button enabled — and the mock **always** returns a wash-sale warning (`oms.ts:54`). Every rebalance ships with an unresolved warning that one person clicks through, with no reason capture.
- **`handleExecute` sends only `{accountIds, taxAware}`** (`RebalancePage.tsx:146-151`) — not the previewed trades. **The advisor approves one trade list and the server regenerates another.** This is a books-and-records problem, not just a bug.
- **Missing from the preview**: before/after allocation comparison, post-trade drift, transaction cost estimate, tracking-error impact. `rebalancing` Step 6 names all four as the decision artefact *and* the compliance artefact. The word "tracking error" does not appear anywhere in the repo outside the benchmark table.
- **`ModelTradeView` has no compliance step at all** — preview goes straight to execute, and its Drift % column is `id.charCodeAt(...) % 5 + 1.2` (`ModelTradeView.tsx:167-169`).
- **Cross-account / household-level optimisation is absent.** `rebalancing` Steps 1, 2 and 5 (household aggregation, asset location, cross-account tax optimisation) are the core of what makes an advisor rebalance different from a robo rebalance. The wizard operates account-by-account.

**Trade ticket.** Fields present are respectable (TIF, extended hours, order types, per-asset-class fields, Greeks card, bond details card, order summary with commission). What's **absent** and would be noticed immediately:
- **Settlement date** (T+1 since May 2024) — decorative text only, wrong for 3 of 5 asset classes.
- **Solicited/unsolicited flag** — zero occurrences in the repo. This is a required order-ticket field.
- **Options approval level** — `sell_to_open` (naked short options) is offered with no account permission check, no margin check, no buying-power validation.
- **Accredited-investor / qualified-purchaser check** — zero occurrences, despite `acc-002` and `acc-006` holding **BXPE (Blackstone private equity)** and the proposal boilerplate itself saying alternatives *"are generally suitable only for qualified investors"* (`proposals.ts:253`).
- **Trade rationale / order note** — the type exists (`archive.ts:14 trade_rationale`) but is not wired into any ticket.
- **Suitability check at the point of trade** — the pre-trade engine never reads the account's risk profile, model or IPS.
- **Cancel/correct, amend, or any order-approval queue.** Orders have a `cancelled` status no UI can produce.
- **No short-sale locate, no PDT check, no buying-power check, no price collar.** `validateTrade` (`validation.ts:31-71`) checks only `> 0` — a limit buy at $0.01 or $99,999 passes.
- **Pre-trade compliance is silently skipped on the main path.** `usePreTradeCheck` is called only from `TradeTicketDialog`; `SingleTradeView` — the only screen that can place options, bonds, funds and crypto — never calls it. The stricter surface is the unchecked one.

**Pre-trade compliance rule coverage** (`pre-trade-compliance`). Present: restricted list, single-name concentration, cash minimum, wash sale, sector concentration. Missing: suitability/risk-profile, IPS constraints, prohibited security for account type (options in an IRA, crypto in a 529, margin in an IRA), accreditation, OFAC/sanctions, employee/access-person pre-clearance, cross-trade and principal-trade rules, fund market-timing rules, block allocation fairness, best-execution/order-type reasonableness.

**Reconciliation** (`reconciliation` skill). The three-way match rate framing (position / cash / transaction) is correct and a nice touch. Missing: **as-of / T-1 date convention**, break **aging buckets**, break **owner/assignment**, break **type taxonomy** (timing vs true break vs pricing), and any concept of an unreconciled item blocking billing or performance calculation. And the four break rows name the wrong accounts (D17).

**Onboarding.** Suitability dimension coverage is genuinely good — time horizon, drawdown reaction, objective, income, experience, % of net worth, liquidity need, volatility comfort. Missing: absolute net worth, tax bracket, dependants, planned withdrawals, existing debt/insurance. The recommended model is a **free-text string** (`'Balanced Growth (60/40)'`) that matches no model ID in the portfolio system, so the suitability output never binds to the portfolio. The "compliance checklist" is three self-attested booleans (`ReviewStep.tsx:101-105`) with no evidence, no timestamp, no attester identity, and no CIP/AML/OFAC screen anywhere.

**What a real advisor would immediately notice is missing entirely:**
1. **An IPS object.** The app references "IPS limits", "IPS review overdue", "IPS concentration limits" — there is **no IPS entity in the codebase**. Every constraint is a global constant. `investment-policy` makes the IPS the source of the bands, the benchmark and the constraints; here it is a word.
2. **Settlement dates and a T+1 lifecycle.** No trade date / settlement date, no pending-settlement cash, no fail tracking.
3. **RMD calculation as a feature.** RMD is narrated in an NBA but there is no RMD engine, no prior-year-end balance, no distribution scheduling, no withholding election — and James Thompson's **inherited IRA (`acc-046`) has its own RMD regime that the app never mentions**.
4. **Held-away accounts as first-class objects.** `heldAwayAUM` is a number on the household. The Williams 401(k) that nba-014 recommends rolling over cannot be inspected, and the wash-sale scan cannot see it — which is exactly the scenario `tax-loss-harvesting` calls the "IRA wash-sale trap".
5. **Performance calculation methodology anywhere.** No composite definition, no cash-flow handling, no Modified Dietz, no gross/net toggle, no as-of date.
6. **Corporate actions, dividends/DRIP settings, cost-basis transfer-in.**
7. **Client-facing report delivery / e-delivery consent** (`client-reporting-delivery`).

## 5. Compliance gaps

### Genuinely required (would be a finding in an SEC exam or a marketing review)

1. **Hypothetical performance shown with no disclosure.** SEC Marketing Rule 206(4)-1(d)(6) treats projections and targeted returns as *hypothetical performance*, requiring policies and procedures reasonably designed to ensure relevance to the recipient, plus disclosure of criteria, assumptions, risks and limitations. The app shows a **Monte Carlo percentile table and histogram** (`RiskAnalyticsPage.tsx:220-289`) with a `10,000 sims / 1Y` badge and **zero disclosure, zero assumptions, zero "not a prediction" language**. Same for the stress scenarios and the sensitivity ladders. `gips-compliance`, `advertising-compliance`.
2. **The proposal's projected return is an unsupported performance claim.** `proposals.ts:120`: *"We project these adjustments will improve your portfolio's expected annual return by approximately **40-80 basis points** while maintaining a comparable level of risk."* This is a **hardcoded string emitted for every client and every template** — it appears identically on a proposal that *reduces* equity 35%→25% and on one that *raises* it 40%→45%. There are no capital-market assumptions anywhere in the repo to support it. No methodology, no assumption set, no time period, no probability. The only hedge sits 148 lines away in a different section. This is the single clearest Marketing Rule exposure in the app. `advertising-compliance`, `proposal-generation`.
3. **Performance shown without net-of-fees labelling or an as-of date.** The Performance tab labels TWR (good) but never states gross vs net, never names the benchmark, never gives period-end dates, and never carries "past performance is not indicative of future results." The proposal boilerplate has all of this (`proposals.ts:247, 268`) — it just never reaches the screens that display returns. `gips-compliance`, `performance-reporting`, `advertising-compliance`.
4. **The rollover recommendation is backwards and is the highest-risk item in the app.** `nbas.ts:186-194`: *"David Williams has $1.2M in a 401(k) at his former employer (Fidelity). **Consolidating could save ~$4,800/year in fees**."* The Williams household is billed at **85 bps** (`billing.ts:12`). Rolling $1.2M into an advisor-managed IRA would **add roughly $10,200/year in advisory fees**, not save $4,800. Rollover recommendations are the specific fact pattern DOL PTE 2020-02 and Reg BI Care Obligation target, and the specific claim ("you'll save money") that gets cited. This should be reframed as a cost *comparison* with both sides shown, plus the required rollover disclosure. `reg-bi`, `fiduciary-standards`, `fee-disclosure`, `conflicts-of-interest`.
5. **Fee disclosure arithmetic is wrong in the client-facing document.** C33: the proposal quotes $40,250/0.805% on $5M when its own tier table gives $45,500/0.91% — a **$5,250/yr understatement** in the fee section of a document delivered to a prospect. `proposals.ts:234` also promises *"No additional trading commissions or transaction fees"* while `trade-fields/shared.tsx:131-136` charges $4.95/equity trade, $0.65/option contract and $1.00/bond, and `oms.ts:109` persists commission on every order. `fee-disclosure`.
6. **The suitability compliance rule is unimplemented and always passes.** `mocks/handlers/proposals.ts:218-250`: rule `cr-001` — *"An aggressive growth portfolio is unsuitable for a conservative-rated client"* — has **no implementation**, despite `draft.clientData.riskTolerance` and `riskScore` sitting on the same object. `cr-002` (concentration) and `cr-005` (IPS consistency) are likewise unimplemented. **No code path can produce `'fail'`**, so the finalisation gate (`ProposalWizard.tsx:51-53`) is unconditionally open, and `ComplianceStep.tsx:82`'s failure banner is unreachable. You can select the Growth template (*"Accumulation-phase clients under 50 with 15+ year time horizons"*) for a 78-year-old conservative client and the wizard reports "All compliance checks passed." `investment-suitability`, `reg-bi`, `advice-standards`.
7. **Books-and-records: AI output is not captured.** The archive module is excellent in *concept* — WORM, `retentionPolicy: '7 years (SEC 17a-4)'`, `aiModel`, `dataSources`, `originalContent` vs `finalContent`, `disposition`. But **nothing writes to it**. `/api/ai/message`, `/api/ai/actions/:id/execute`, `/api/ai/generate-document` and `/api/ai/templates/:id/execute` all return content with no archive write; the archive is populated only by hand-authored static data. AI-drafted client communications and AI trade recommendations are therefore un-retained. Advisers Act Rule 204-2 requires retention of communications recommending securities. `books-and-records`, `examination-readiness`.
8. **Batch NBA dismissal bypasses the compliance control that single-dismissal enforces.** `nba.ts:274-294` loops `nba.dismissed = true` over every ID, skipping the `nonDismissible` / `supervisorOverride` check at `nba.ts:104-124` and writing **no audit trail**. A compliance-flagged item (large wire source-of-funds, IPS review overdue) can be cleared in bulk with no record. `books-and-records`, `examination-readiness`.
9. **The advisor can switch off the compliance category and re-weight the queue toward revenue, with no record.** `NBASettingsTab.tsx:286-292` lets a user toggle `compliance` off the feed entirely; `:49-70` ships a **"Revenue" preset** (`impact 35, confidence 30, urgency 10`) with a live preview of the re-ranked queue. No change log, no supervisory notification. `conflicts-of-interest`, `examination-readiness`.

### Adds credibility (not strictly required, but expected of a serious product)

- Gross/net toggle and a stated fee assumption on every performance surface.
- Benchmark named on the chart and in the risk panel (beta against *what*?).
- A composite/GIPS-adjacent note where firm-level performance is shown.
- Reg BI / Form CRS delivery tracking in onboarding alongside the ADV Part 2 reference.
- Best-execution documentation on the order blotter.
- Fee waiver/discount audit trail (the Billing tab exists but is empty of data).
- Trade rationale capture on the ticket — the type already exists.
- Political-contribution / pay-to-play (Rule 206(4)-5) and gifts & entertainment logs, given the CRM depth.
- Tax-equivalent yield on the municipal bond details card.

## 6. AI framing in a regulated context

**Does the AI layer make domain sense?** The *taxonomy* does. The NBA categories (rebalancing / tax_management / planning / risk / client_service / compliance / growth) map to how advisors actually triage, and the five-factor score (urgency, impact, efficiency, relationship, confidence) is close to what `next-best-action` describes. Grouping seven drifted accounts into a single batch action is exactly the right ergonomic. The `nonDismissible` + `escalationLevel` + `auditTrail` treatment of compliance NBAs is genuinely thoughtful and is the best compliance idea in the product.

**But the ranking is not defensible as built.** Composites are not reproducible from their components under any weighting (D36), so the ordering cannot be explained to a client or an examiner. And the ranking is mutable by the advisor toward revenue with no record. In a fiduciary context, *"why did the system show you this and not that"* is an answerable question — here it isn't.

**Are these the actions an advisor would want ranked this way?** Mostly yes, with two exceptions. A **critical, non-dismissible AML item with a 48-hour deadline** (nba-013, `composite: 69`) ranks **below a batch rebalance** (nba-001, 77) and a **birthday card** ranks at 69 — tied with it. Deadline-bearing regulatory items should dominate the queue structurally, not compete on a blended score. Second, the highest-ranked items are the ones with the largest `estimatedImpact`, and `estimatedImpact` is systematically inflated (below).

### Specific risks

1. **`estimatedImpact` conflates gross loss with tax benefit.** nba-002 shows **$224,000** as the "impact" of harvesting INTC. $224K is the **realised loss**, not the benefit; at a blended 30–40% the benefit is $67–90K. The dashboard's TLH card makes the same 37% flat-rate error. Presenting the gross loss as impact overstates by ~3× — and these figures roll into the NBA effectiveness module's "estimated revenue impact". `tax-loss-harvesting`, `advertising-compliance`.
2. **A rebalancing delegation to an AI agent ships enabled by default.** `settings.ts:173-180`: `dr-001 'Routine rebalancing under $500K'`, condition *"Account value < $500K AND drift > threshold AND no wash sale conflict"*, `delegateTo: 'AI Agent'`, `delegationType: 'ai_agent'`, **`active: true`**. Meanwhile `dr-004 'Tax-loss harvesting execution'` — the one with `delegationType: 'both'` (AI + advisor review) — ships **disabled**. The defaults are exactly inverted from what a CCO would set. Worse, the conditions are **free-text strings never parsed by any engine**, so the stated guardrails ("no wash sale conflict") are decorative.
3. **The permission matrix has no ceiling.** `PermissionMatrixCard.tsx:78-91` lets any user set **any** action type — including `trade_suggestion` and `compliance_notes` — to `auto_approve`, widen `dataScope` to `full_book`, and set `executionSchedule` to `always` (24/7), with no supervisor gate, no confirmation and no change log. A dead settings object at `settings.ts:8-14` contains `executeTrades: false` but is read by nothing (and has a typo'd key, `scheduleMetings`). The guardrail is inert data.
4. **The AI misstates its own guardrails.** `ai.ts:118`: *"I … require approval before executing trades or sending client communications."* That is false whenever the matrix is set otherwise — and it can be, by anyone.
5. **"Execute Trade" is a lie in the other direction.** `TradeSuggestionCard.tsx:39` labels the button **"Execute Trade"**; it actually pre-fills a ticket (`AIChatPanel.tsx:272,328-338`). Overclaiming autonomy in the label is its own problem, and there is **no confirmation, no suitability check and no disclaimer between the AI's recommendation and the pre-filled order**.
6. **There is not one disclaimer anywhere near AI output.** No "AI-generated — verify before use", no accuracy caveat, no "not investment advice". The only marker is a purple `Sparkles` badge (`ChatBubble.tsx:31-33`). Yet the AI speaks in flat declaratives that are specific securities recommendations: *"I recommend a phased liquidation over 90 days paired with tax-loss harvesting"* (`ai.ts:237`), *"Replace with IXUS after 31-day wash sale window"* (`ai.ts:268`), *"improving after-tax yield by approximately 0.3% annually"* (`ai.ts:81`), and named buy/sell tickets on VTI, BND, AAPL, QQQ, VXUS with quantities and dollar values. The `customDisclaimer` field ships **empty** (`settings.ts:25`) and applies only to proposals.
7. **Citations exist but are de-emphasised and incomplete.** They collapse by default (`ChatBubble.tsx:15`) behind 11px tertiary text, they are system labels rather than verifiable references, and **trade suggestions carry no citations at all** — the outputs with the highest consequence have the least provenance.
8. **AI-authored suitability conclusions with no basis.** `handlers/ai.ts:374`: *"…has an aggressive risk profile (72/100). Current allocation appears aligned with stated tolerance."* That is a suitability determination, unsourced, un-archived, and rendered as fact.
9. **`sr-004 'Post-trade compliance review'`** (`settings.ts:123-134`) — *"Run post-trade compliance check against IPS constraints and concentration limits"* on trades over $100K. Post-trade review is a real control, but pairing it with an auto-approve matrix and an AI rebalancing delegation means the *only* IPS check in the system happens **after** execution.

### Recommended framing

- **Reframe the AI as a drafting and triage layer, never an execution layer, and make the product enforce it.** Remove `auto_approve` as an option for `trade_suggestion` and `compliance_notes`; make those modes structurally unavailable rather than merely unselected. Ship `dr-001` **disabled** and `dr-004` (AI + advisor review) **enabled** — the inverse of today.
- **Add a standing hedge to every AI surface**, not just proposals: *"AI-generated. Review for accuracy before use. Not a recommendation until reviewed and approved by your advisor."* One line, six words of code, and it changes how the whole product reads to a compliance audience.
- **Write every AI output to the archive that already exists.** The WORM/17a-4 model is already built; wiring `/api/ai/message` and `/api/ai/generate-document` into it converts the biggest compliance gap into the biggest compliance differentiator.
- **Make the NBA score reproducible and show the weights.** A tooltip that renders `0.25×urgency + 0.30×impact + …= 77` turns an opaque ranking into an auditable one. Log weight changes.
- **Rank deadline-bearing regulatory items above scored items structurally** — a separate, always-top band rather than a competing composite.
- **Relabel `estimatedImpact`.** Say "harvestable loss: $224K / est. tax benefit: $67–90K", not "$224K impact".

## 7. Highest-credibility-per-unit-effort fixes

Ranked by (credibility gained) ÷ (effort). Items 1–6 are each under an hour and each removes a five-second kill.

1. **Fix the fee unit and reconcile the three fee numbers.** Change `EFFECTIVE FEE 0.85 bps` → `85 bps`, and derive it as `Σ household fees ÷ Σ AUM` (79.6 bps) so Book, Billing and the fee schedule agree. *One label and one division. This is the most damaging single character in the app.* (C29)
2. **Make household AUM = Σ of its accounts, FX-converted.** Delete the hand-typed `managedAUM` / `totalAUM` fields and compute them. This alone closes D1, D13, D23 and the $14.6M book gap, and makes Households, Book, Billing and Client Detail agree. *Delete 26 constants, add one reducer.* (D1)
3. **Scale VaR and stress by account, and label VaR with horizon + confidence + method.** Even keeping the numbers fake: `var95 = totalValue × 0.059` with the caption "1-month, 95%, parametric". A reviewer flipping the account selector currently sees identical risk for a $100K IRA and a $12M trust — that is the fastest possible way to lose a domain audience. (C5, C9)
4. **Add the "Total Interaction Effect" card and label the attribution model.** Four lines in `AttributionPage.tsx`, plus a caption: *"Brinson-Fachler, single-period, arithmetic. Benchmark: [name]. Gross of fees."* The three summary cards then add up, which is the first thing anyone checks. Bonus: reconcile the attribution total to the benchmark table's active return. (C2, C3)
5. **Exclude funds from the single-name concentration limit.** Add `isFund` to `Position` and skip the 10% test for ETFs/mutual funds — or apply a separate fund-level limit. Right now the module's headline output is "VTI is an IPS breach", which reads as not knowing what an index fund is. NVDA at 13.9% then becomes the genuine, and much more compelling, story. (C23)
6. **Rename the wash-sale calendar to 61 days and anchor it on loss sales.** Feed it from realised sells (the data already exists in `RECENT_SELLS` and `oms.ts` fills) rather than `purchaseDate`. Even with two hand-authored sale events it becomes a real feature instead of an inverted one. (C10)
7. **Split the TLH tax rate by holding period.** `lot.holdingPeriod === 'short' ? 0.37 : 0.238` (or expose the rate as a client attribute). Two ternaries. Currently the app shows a 37% ordinary rate next to a "Long" badge on the same row — a domain audience reads that as not knowing the difference. (C11)
8. **Make tax lots partition their positions and stop randomising them.** Seed the generator, and derive lot cost basis so `Σ lot.costBasis = position.costBasis` and `Σ lot.quantity = position.quantity`. This also makes the cost-basis comparison table deterministic and lets `washSaleRestricted` mean something. (C12, D33)
9. **Fix the 2-year Treasury and the CA muni yields, and drop `yieldToWorst` unless it differs.** A bond at a discount must yield more than its coupon; that is the single most basic relationship in fixed income and it is inverted on the first row of the bond list. (C19, C20, C21)
10. **Invert the option gamma formula and scale theta by time value.** `gamma ∝ (1 − |2Δ − 1|)` peaking ATM; `theta ≈ −timeValue/DTE`. Nobody who trades options will look past a gamma surface that is lowest at the money. (C15, C16)
11. **Read the model's own `driftTolerance`, and make the threshold input actually filter.** The data is already there and already displayed on the Model Governance page — it is simply never used. This turns model governance from decoration into a control. (C24, C25)
12. **Fix the four reconciliation break account names.** Four string edits. A reconciliation screen that misnames every account it reconciles is self-refuting. (D17)
13. **Reframe the rollover NBA as a cost comparison, not a saving.** *"$1.2M held-away 401(k). Plan cost ~0.45%; our schedule 0.85%. Rollover raises annual cost ~$4,800 — evaluate against consolidated tax management, planning and rebalancing. PTE 2020-02 disclosure required."* This converts the most legally exposed item in the app into a demonstration of fiduciary literacy — which is a *stronger* showcase moment than the original. (Compliance §4)
14. **Delete or ground the "40–80 basis points" proposal claim.** Either remove it, or compute it from a stated capital-market assumption set with a visible methodology note. As a hardcoded string it is the clearest Marketing Rule exposure in the product. (C33, Compliance §2)
15. **Add one disclosure line to each analytics surface.** Performance: *"Time-weighted, net of advisory fees, as of 25 Feb 2026. Benchmark: 60/40 S&P 500 / Bloomberg Agg. Past performance is not indicative of future results."* Monte Carlo/stress: *"Hypothetical. Not a prediction. Assumptions: 6% expected return, 12% volatility, 1-year horizon, fees not deducted."* Two sentences that signal you know which regime each screen falls under.
16. **Wire AI output into the existing archive, and add a one-line AI hedge.** The WORM/17a-4 archive is already built and is the strongest compliance concept in the app; it just isn't connected. Connecting it plus a six-word caveat on `ChatBubble` and `TradeSuggestionCard` converts the AI layer from a liability into the thing a compliance-literate reviewer remembers. (Compliance §7, AI §6)
17. **Reconcile the dashboard's self-contradictions.** Derive `pendingTasks` from `nbas.length`, `alertCount` from the compliance alerts, and use one net-flow number. Three lines, and it removes three contradictions from the first screen anyone sees. (D21)
18. **Freeze the clock.** Use `MOCK_NOW` for the dashboard greeting instead of `new Date()`. The landing page currently says July while the data says February. (D20)

**If only three things get done:** #1 (fee unit), #2 (household roll-up), #3 (per-account risk). Those three are what a practitioner checks in the first ninety seconds, and all three are arithmetic, not architecture.
